• Read the FITS: 32-bit IEEE float, 3 image planes, rows stored top-down. Pixel values were already on a 0–1 scale, and the data spans 0.18413 to 1.00000.
• No colour filter array: the file carries 3 separate colour planes (NAXIS3 = 3) and is already demosaiced. No debayer applied.
• Data is already non-linear: the header records Autostretch and Histogram Transf. and Midtones transfer, the measured median sits at 0.2467. Stretch reduced accordingly — black point taken at the 0.050% percentile instead of a sigma clip, and each channel’s median held where it already sits, so existing tonal placement is preserved.
• Background extraction: measured the sky in 96 boxes of 25 pixels on a 12 × 8 grid and used 81 of them (15 brighter than the background were left out). A thin-plate spline through those points is the model that was removed.
    Each channel got its own model median back as a pedestal (R 0.26322, G 0.24486, B 0.22965), so the background level is preserved and only its variation was removed.
    The ratio between channels is therefore unchanged: no colour grading, no white balance, nothing was decided about the colour of the sky.
• Colour calibration: the sky background was levelled between channels (R 0.017263, G -0.001086, B -0.016178 subtracted, additively, so only the floor moved).
    Stellar gains: the faintest star median is 0.407718, less than 3x the sky pedestal (0.246154); a ratio taken above the sky is unstable when the star is barely above it.
    Star selection is a brightness cut, not star detection: bright unsaturated pixels are predominantly stars in a star field. Pixels whose neighbourhood is also mostly selected are rejected as extended, which removes a galaxy core but would also remove a star field dense enough to look like one.
• Autostretch — midtones transfer function (PixInsight STF / Siril autostretch), target background held at the luminance median. Applied LINKED: one curve, derived from the luminance, and every channel multiplied by the same number. Highlights untouched at 1.000.
    Luminance  median 0.24593  MADN 0.00441  →  shadows 0.23743  midtones 0.03341
    Because one curve governs all three channels, the ratio between them is unchanged by construction. Measured on this frame, the largest change to any channel ratio was 4.4e-16 across 539,709 pixels — float rounding, nothing else. In the highlights, R/G 0.9805 → 0.9808 and B/G 1.0145 → 1.0141.
    8,434 pixels came out above 1.0 in one channel. All three were divided by their own maximum rather than the bright channel being clipped on its own: clipping one channel changes the colour of the pixel, dividing takes it to white and keeps it.
    0.056% of pixels were clipped by the transfer itself — luminance at or below the black point, or at or above 1.0. That is a different count from the line above: this one is the curve, that one is the overflow.
• Output: 8-bit sRGB, full resolution, no resampling. Rounded with ±0.5 of a level of dither, from the fixed seed 20260906, which breaks the banding a subtracted surface would otherwise leave. The seed is fixed, so the same file always produces the same image.
• Not applied: noise reduction, sharpening, saturation, deconvolution, star removal, colour grading, or any AI or generative step.
