• Read the FITS: 16-bit unsigned integer (BZERO 32768), one image plane, rows stored bottom-up and flipped back the right way up. Pixel values rescaled onto 0–1 from the 0 to 65535 range they were stored in, where the data spans 0.00780 to 0.31510.
• Colour filter array: BAYERPAT = GRBG read from the header, and its green sites already match the main diagonal the pixels show. Measured lattice contrast 11.1%, neighbour ratio 3.23 H / 2.73 V.
• Debayered with bilinear interpolation to full-resolution RGB.
• Data is linear: median 0.01100, no stretch recorded in the header. Full autostretch applied.
• Background extraction: measured the sky in 84 boxes of 25 pixels on a 12 × 7 grid and used 73 of them (11 brighter than the background were left out). A thin-plate spline through those points is the model that was removed.
    Each channel got its own model median back as a pedestal (R 0.01223, G 0.01092, B 0.00981), so the background level is preserved and only its variation was removed.
    The ratio between channels is therefore unchanged: no colour grading, no white balance, nothing was decided about the colour of the sky.
• Colour calibration: the sky background was levelled between channels (R 0.001246, G -0.000073, B -0.001173 subtracted, additively, so only the floor moved), and the colour balance was measured from 29,252 star pixels in your own frame — red ×1.007, green ×1.000, blue ×0.944.
    Pixels between 0.01254 and 0.85 in luminance were taken as stars; 818 were left out as extended source, being pixels whose neighbourhood is also mostly lit — a galaxy body, not stars.
    The ratio was taken above the sky, not against the raw pixel: the sky is a level common to all three channels and leaving it in drags every ratio toward 1. Star medians R 0.01667, G 0.01671, B 0.01705; above a sky of 0.01099 that is R 0.00568, G 0.00572, B 0.00606.
    Checked for stability before applying: a 10% error in the sky estimate would move the gains by at most 1.30% (in blue). A ratio taken above the sky is only worth applying if it barely moves when the sky estimate does.
    Nothing here was a preference: the numbers came from the stars you photographed. Star colour is the reference because sky has no colour of its own to measure against. No catalogue was consulted, no astrometry was solved, and nothing left this machine.
    Star selection is a brightness cut, not star detection: bright unsaturated pixels are predominantly stars in a star field. Pixels whose neighbourhood is also mostly selected are rejected as extended, which removes a galaxy core but would also remove a star field dense enough to look like one.
• Autostretch — midtones transfer function (PixInsight STF / Siril autostretch), shadow clip -2.80σ, target background 0.085 — that is 22 of 255. Applied LINKED: one curve, derived from the luminance, and every channel multiplied by the same number. Highlights untouched at 1.000.

    Because one curve governs all three channels, the ratio between them is unchanged by construction. Measured on this frame, the largest change to any channel ratio was 1.8e-15 across 2,073,545 pixels — float rounding, nothing else. In the highlights, R/G 0.9891 → 0.9947 and B/G 0.9593 → 0.9614.
    1,940 pixels came out above 1.0 in one channel. All three were divided by their own maximum rather than the bright channel being clipped on its own: clipping one channel changes the colour of the pixel, dividing takes it to white and keeps it.
    0.002% of pixels were clipped by the transfer itself — luminance at or below the black point, or at or above 1.0. That is a different count from the line above: this one is the curve, that one is the overflow.
• Selective saturation: chroma was scaled by up to ×1.450 (the ceiling in use is ×1.45), strongest where the signal is well above the noise and rolled off in the highlights so a bright core does not turn into a flat disc of colour.
    The mask is driven by signal-to-noise, never by brightness: a pixel is left alone below 3.0σ above the sky and gets the full factor above 25.0σ. On this frame the sky sits at 0.08542 with a noise of 0.02778, so 2,032,290 pixels (98.0%) were not touched at all. Average factor across the frame: ×1.004.
    Hue was not changed — all three channels were scaled by the same number, so the direction of the colour is untouched and only its strength moved. Measured on this frame, the largest hue change was 8.4e-14 of a turn across 2,073,563 pixels. Background chroma noise grew -0.00%.
    2,650 pixels came out above 1.0 in one channel and 138 below 0.0. All three channels were moved together in both cases rather than the offending one being clipped on its own: clipping one channel changes the colour of the pixel, which is the one thing this step promises not to do.
    This is the one step here that is a preference rather than a measurement, and it says so. The mask and the roll-off are measured — they are what stops a preference from colouring noise or flattening a core — but how much colour you want is a choice, and it was made for you at ×1.45.
